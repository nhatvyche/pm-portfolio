04 Key Decisions

What I chose — and what
I deliberately said no to

The hardest PM skill isn't deciding what to build. It's deciding what not to build and defending that with clear reasoning.

Local-first architecture — all data stays on user's machine

Cloud server processing user's meeting notes

Freelancers have confidential client data. Privacy = trust = retention.

Notion-only data source for v1

Zoom + Teams + Gmail + Notion all at once

Scope creep kills MVPs. Validate the core loop first.

Pre-meeting briefing only (pull feature)

Post-meeting follow-up drafts in v1

Push features are harder. Earn trust with pull first, then earn permission to push.

Recency weighting + keyword detection for context freshness

Show all notes from past 6 months equally

Old resolved topics are noise. The briefing must earn trust every time it fires.

07 Learnings So Far

What this project
has already taught me

01

The best products connect tools, not replace them

Zoom does its job. Notion does its job. Calendar does its job. Nobody connects them forward-looking for the freelancer. That gap IS the product.

02

Data quality is a product problem, not a tech problem

Recall's output is only as good as the user's Notion notes. That's a behavior design challenge — not something you solve with a better algorithm.

03

Saying no is the hardest and most important skill

Post-meeting follow-up, team features, Windows version — all cut from v1. Each cut made the remaining scope more credible and shippable.

04

Privacy is a feature, not a constraint

Choosing local-first wasn't just a safety decision — it became a core marketing angle. "Recall never sees your data" removes the biggest objection upfront.
